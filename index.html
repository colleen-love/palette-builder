        Mixing

        From your palette

          Each medium (watercolor, gouache, oil and acrylic) has its own paints, palette, stored palette, filters and mixes. Switch between them from the menu next to the title.

          Mixes are simulated with Mixbox (Secret Weapons, CC BY-NC 4.0), the same pigment-mixing model artistpigments.org uses for its mixing paths, starting from each paint's measured masstone, the same color as its dot on the wheel. Real tinting strength isn't known, so a strong paint will dominate more in real mixes than here. Mixes leave out water and white. Treat mixes as a preview and confirm on paper or canvas.

          Color data and paint properties: artistpigments.org (CC BY-NC 4.0), mostly handmade masstone samples; a few paints use the manufacturer's published values or a printed color chart, and say so. Lightfastness is converted from each brand's own scale (ASTM, AA–C, stars) to a common I–IV, and staining to non, semi or staining. Filters only appear when the current medium's data has values for them. Where a watercolor brand doesn't publish staining or granulation, the value is inferred from how other brands rate the same pigment and shown with a hollow dot (○) instead of a filled one (●). The Inferred filter can leave these out, and pigments that brands rate differently stay unknown. Price series are each brand's own. Reach is the area your palette covers in the a*b* plane, traced along the simulated mixing curve between every pair of your paints rather than straight lines. It's a guide, not a measurement. The wheel doesn't show lightness; use the Lightness filter (0 is black, 100 is white) to narrow by it. Not affiliated with any paint maker.
